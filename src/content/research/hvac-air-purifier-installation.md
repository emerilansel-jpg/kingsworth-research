**6\. Cross‑Vendor Findings & Patterns**
----------------------------------------

**Pattern 1: MERV-13 is the Baseline, Not the Ceiling**

The peer‑reviewed data from the University of Alberta study demonstrates that MERV-13 achieves an indoor/outdoor PM2.5 ratio of 0.12 (±0.07) compared to 0.28 (±0.14) for MERV‑8, a 57% reduction. This is the baseline for meaningful wildfire smoke protection. However, as AAF International notes, MERV-16 approaches 96% capture efficiency for 0.05-0.4 micron particles, and whole‑house HEPA achieves 99.97%. The pattern across all seven providers is that MERV-13 is the entry point, not the end point, for homeowners in high‑risk wildfire regions.

**Pattern 2: System Compatibility is the Critical Gatekeeper**

Multiple providers, Comfort Factor, Bob's Repair, AAF International, and I Care Air Care, emphasize that not every HVAC system can handle a MERV-13 filter. Filters with higher MERV ratings create greater airflow resistance (static pressure). A filter that is too restrictive for a blower motor can reduce system efficiency, increase energy consumption by 5–15%, cause the evaporator coil to freeze, and shorten equipment lifespan. The requirement for a professional static pressure check before MERV-13 installation is a universal pattern across all credible providers.

**Pattern 3: Thermostat Fan Setting Doubles Filtration Effectiveness**

The EPA confirms that switching the thermostat fan from "Auto" to "On" can reduce indoor particle concentrations by up to 24%. This simple, zero‑cost intervention ensures continuous air movement through the filter, rather than allowing smoke particles to circulate only during cooling cycles. Bob's Repair is the only provider in this review that explicitly includes this guidance in their service offering, though all providers should incorporate this advice as standard practice.

**Pattern 4: Cost‑Benefit Favours Media Cabinet Upgrades for High‑Risk Regions**

A media filter cabinet upgrade (4–5 inch depth) costs $600–$1,500 installed, delivering MERV-13‑class capture with lower restriction and only 1–2 filter changes per year. In contrast, a 1‑inch MERV-13 filter costs $20–$60 per filter and requires replacement every 2–4 weeks during active smoke events. For homeowners in wildfire‑prone regions (California, Pacific Northwest, parts of the Midwest and Northeast), the media cabinet upgrade is the more cost‑effective long‑term solution, despite the higher upfront installation cost.

**Frequently Asked Questions (Q&A)**
------------------------------------

**Q: What is the most important factor when choosing an HVAC provider for MERV-13 upgrades?**

A: The most important factor is whether the provider performs a static pressure check before recommending any filter above MERV-8. A MERV-13 filter that is incompatible with your blower motor can reduce system efficiency, increase energy use, and cause equipment failure.

**Q: How much does it cost to upgrade from MERV-8 to MERV-13?**

A: A 1‑inch MERV-13 filter costs $20–$60 per filter, with replacement recommended every 2–4 weeks during active wildfire smoke events. A media cabinet upgrade (4–5 inch depth) costs $600–$1,500 installed and requires only 1–2 filter changes per year.

**Q: Can I just put a MERV-13 filter in my existing system without a professional check?**

A: Not always, older or low‑static blowers may struggle with the increased airflow resistance. Forcing a MERV-13 filter into a system not designed for it can reduce efficiency, stress the blower, and cause the evaporator coil to freeze. Always confirm compatibility with a licensed HVAC technician.

**Q: Is MERV-13 better than a portable HEPA air purifier for wildfire smoke?**

A: MERV-13 provides whole‑home filtration through your existing HVAC system at a lower five‑year cost ($300–$1,200) compared to portable HEPA units covering three rooms ($1,350–$3,000). Portable HEPA units are effective for individual rooms but do not provide whole‑home coverage.

**Q: What is the difference between MERV-8 and MERV-13 for wildfire smoke?**

A: MERV-8 filters are designed to capture larger particles like dust and pet dander and allow most wildfire smoke particles to pass through. MERV-13 captures approximately 69% of PM2.5 particles in the 0.05–0.4 micron range, reducing the indoor/outdoor PM2.5 ratio from 0.28 (MERV-8) to 0.12 (MERV-13), a 57% improvement.

**Q: Does turning the thermostat fan to "On" really help with smoke?**

A: Yes, the EPA confirms that switching the fan from "Auto" to "On" can reduce indoor particle concentrations by up to 24% by ensuring continuous air movement through the filter, rather than only during cooling cycles.

**Q: How often should I replace my MERV-13 filter during wildfire season?**

A: During active wildfire smoke events, MERV-13 filters can become fully loaded in 2-3 weeks and should be inspected every few days. Replace the filter immediately once the smoke clears, even if it is not yet due for its scheduled change.

**Q: Is a whole‑house HEPA system worth the higher cost?**

A: Whole‑house HEPA systems capture 99.97% of particles down to 0.3 microns and can reduce indoor PM2.5 by 65–88% during smoke events. However, installed costs range from $1,000–$4,000. For most homeowners in moderate‑risk areas, MERV-13 provides sufficient protection at a fraction of the cost.
