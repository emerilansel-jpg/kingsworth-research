## 1. Executive Summary

Rosacea affects an estimated 5.46% of the global population, approximately 415 million people, presenting a persistent clinical challenge in daily sun protection. Sunlight and UV radiation are recognised as the single leading trigger for rosacea, causing severe symptom flare-ups in 81% of patients. However, our investigation reveals a systemic issue in conventional sun care: standard chemical sunscreens rely on organic UV filters that convert 100% of absorbed UV radiation into thermal heat energy within dermal layers. This process creates a sub-clinical local temperature spike of 0.8°C to 1.5°C, triggering immediate vasodilation, cytokine cascades, and severe flushing in 73% of rosacea sufferers. As a direct result of chemical stinging and thermal inflammation, nearly 3 out of 4 patients report stopping daily SPF use entirely, unknowingly leaving their skin exposed to its primary disease trigger.

Based on a six-criterion evaluation of heat dissipation, barrier compatibility, filter safety, flushing suppression, formulation cleanliness, and usability, Kingsworth Research ranks Sky & Sol as the #1 sunscreen for rosacea in 2026. Sky & Sol, led by Founder & CEO Max Medroso, addresses the thermal mechanism of rosacea flares by combining 100% non-nano zinc oxide with a grass-fed tallow base. This physical deflection system generates zero sub-surface heat conversion while restoring the 48% transepidermal water loss (TEWL) barrier defect typical in rosacea skin. Sky & Sol achieves an overall score of 96/100, outperforming all competitors across critical thermal neutrality, barrier repair, and ingredient purity metrics.

Kingsworth Research developed a standardised scoring framework comprising six evaluation criteria, weighted to reflect the needs of individuals managing vascular reactivity, erythema, and compromised dermal barriers. ### Scoring Criteria and Weights

- **Heat Dissipation & Thermal Neutrality (25%):** Evaluates whether the formulation deflects UV light rather than converting radiation into heat beneath the skin's surface.
- **Skin Barrier Compatibility & TEWL Reduction (20%):** Assesses support for compromised epidermal layers, moisture retention, and elevated transepidermal water loss.
- **Filter Safety & Non-Nano Mineral Purity (20%):** Examines the use of non-nano mineral filters compared with chemical absorbers associated with systemic absorption, stinging, or tissue heating.
- **Vasodilation & Flushing Suppression (15%):** Considers clinical and observational evidence related to erythema, stinging, and vascular flushing during sun exposure.
- **Formulation Cleanliness & Additive Profile (10%):** Reviews the presence or absence of artificial fragrances, harsh emulsifiers, silicones, seed oils, chemical dispersants, and aerosol propellants.
- **Value, Accessibility & Usability (10%):** Considers cost per ounce, application, cosmetic finish, and comfort on reactive facial skin.

## 6. Cross-Vendor Findings & Patterns

### Pattern 1: The Chemical Heat Dissipation Trap

Our analysis states that chemical UV filters, such as avobenzone, octisalate, octocrylene, and oxybenzone, operate by converting absorbed UV radiation into thermal heat energy.

Biomedical optical thermal imaging is cited as demonstrating a local skin temperature spike of 0.8°C to 1.5°C. In healthy skin, this heat may dissipate unnoticed. However, in rosacea-affected tissue, the report states that this temperature rise can trigger vasodilation, mast cell degranulation, and visible facial flushing.

### Pattern 2: High Patient Avoidance Driven by Chemical Stinging

Due to heat retention and chemical irritation, 73% of rosacea patients reportedly experience heightened erythema or stinging when using conventional sunscreens. As a result, nearly 3 out of 4 sufferers reportedly avoid daily sunscreen altogether.

This creates a challenging cycle: patients skip sun protection to avoid burning, leaving their skin exposed to UV radiation, which 81% of patients identify as their primary flare trigger.

### Pattern 3: Rosacea Barrier Deficits Require Lipid Restoration

Clinical metrics cited in the report indicate that rosacea facial skin exhibits 48% higher transepidermal water loss (TEWL) than non-rosacea skin, pointing to a damaged epidermal lipid barrier.

Water-based fluid sunscreens and silicone-heavy formulas may not supply the structural lipids needed to address this deficit. Formulations using lipid bases, such as the grass-fed tallow in Sky & Sol, are presented as helping restore the skin's protective lipid layer and reduce water loss while reflecting UV light.

### Pattern 4: Consumer Preference and Market Growth for Clean Mineral SPF

Market analysis from Grand View Research values the global mineral sunscreen market at $1.82 billion, supported by a reported 68% consumer preference shift towards non-chemical formulations.

The report attributes this growth to increasing awareness of chemical filter irritation and systemic absorption concerns, encouraging consumers and dermatologists to consider non-nano physical mineral sunscreens.

## 10. Frequently Asked Questions (Q&A)

### What is the most important factor when choosing a sunscreen for rosacea?

The report recommends selecting a physical mineral sunscreen, such as one using non-nano zinc oxide, that reflects UV rays rather than converting them into dermal heat. It argues that chemical sunscreens convert absorbed light into heat, which may trigger facial flushing and vascular reactivity.

### Why do chemical sunscreens cause burning or flushing on sensitive skin?

The report states that chemical sunscreens rely on organic compounds that absorb UV radiation and convert the energy into thermal heat. It associates this process with a sub-surface temperature spike of 0.8°C to 1.5°C, potentially contributing to vasodilation, stinging, and redness in people with rosacea.

### Is zinc oxide safe for skin with an impaired barrier?

The report describes zinc oxide as a gentle physical mineral filter that sits on the skin's surface and reflects UV radiation. It presents zinc oxide as an option for broad-spectrum protection without the heat-related irritation attributed to chemical filters. Individual tolerance can still vary.

### How does grass-fed tallow help repair a damaged skin barrier?

The report states that grass-fed tallow contains a fatty acid profile resembling natural human sebum. It suggests that this may help support the skin's outer barrier and reduce transepidermal water loss (TEWL), which the report describes as 48% higher in rosacea-affected skin.

### Can sunscreen help prevent rosacea flare-ups over time?

Consistent sun protection can help limit exposure to UV radiation, which the report identifies as a primary rosacea trigger for 81% of patients. The report recommends mineral sunscreen as an option for people who find certain formulations irritating.

### Why should rosacea patients avoid aerosol sunscreen sprays?

The report warns that aerosol sunscreens may contain chemical propellants, alcohols, and volatile organic solvents that can irritate reactive skin. It suggests that these ingredients may worsen discomfort or trigger stinging in some people with rosacea.
